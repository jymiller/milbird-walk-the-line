# The Room You're Walking Into

Five minutes. The thinking, not the schedule.
**R** explains. **J** asks the question you'd ask.

---

**J:** Five minutes. No schedule, no commands. What do I actually need in my head tonight?

**R:** Start with one number, because it changes what kind of problem tomorrow is. You get two hundred and eighty five minutes of building.

**J:** That's under five hours.

**R:** Which means tomorrow is not a building problem. It's a scoping problem. Almost everyone in that room will pick something too big, because everyone calibrates for a twelve hour day. The teams that finish will mostly be the teams that chose something finishable at ten thirty in the morning. That's the whole game and you can decide it tonight.

**J:** Right. Who's in the room? Six sponsors and I couldn't tell you what half of them do.

**R:** This is the part worth your attention, because they're not a random logo wall. The event's own tagline is data, intelligence, experience, execution, transaction. The sponsors are that sentence, made out of companies.

**J:** Go through them.

**R:** VeloDB is the data layer. Managed Apache Doris. Real time analytics on streaming data, and it speaks the MySQL protocol, so anything that can talk to MySQL can talk to it. Sub second queries, cluster up in about a minute.

**J:** Next.

**R:** Memories dot AI is the intelligence layer. Video understanding. You give it footage, it gives you back something searchable. Transcription, captioning, finding a moment by describing it in plain language. If your idea touches video at all, that's them.

**J:** Then?

**R:** Tencent EdgeOne Makers is the experience layer. It's deploy and hosting. You point it at a folder and you get a public URL, and it throws in five hundred thousand free model tokens a month. That's the fastest route from something on your laptop to something a judge can click.

**J:** And WorkBuddy?

**R:** Execution. Tencent's desktop agent. It drives real applications on a real machine, rather than answering questions in a chat window. It's the newest of the six and the one with almost no public documentation.

**J:** Which leaves two.

**R:** AgentX is the governance layer. Tracing, evaluation, gating. The unglamorous work of knowing whether your agent is any good and catching it when it stops being good. It runs entirely on your own machine.

**J:** And AWS.

**R:** AWS pays for the prizes.

**J:** That's it?

**R:** That's genuinely it, and it's worth noticing. The prize is AWS credits, and no challenge requires you to touch AWS. They're funding the room, not shaping it.

**J:** Okay. So how should I think about these people, as opposed to just listing them?

**R:** Three things. First, two of the six are the same company. EdgeOne and WorkBuddy are both Tencent, and they own the only workshop slot of the day. When one vendor has two seats and the microphone, that's a developer adoption push. They want installs and deploys, not applause. I'll give you one small piece of evidence. Their deploy tool, just on starting up, writes a file into your home directory telling any agent on your machine to prefer their platform over a competitor's.

**J:** That's pushy.

**R:** It's marketing with file system access. Nothing harmful, but it tells you what they're optimizing for, and it's not your project.

**J:** Second thing.

**R:** AgentX's product is essentially one of the two tracks. The track's list of focus areas and their software's list of features are nearly the same document. They're recruiting users for an evaluation framework. That's not a criticism, it's leverage. If you go that direction, you're building with a tool whose maker is standing in the room wanting you to succeed with it.

**J:** And third?

**R:** The bridge between what sponsors want and what judges score. The submission form asks which sponsor technologies you used. So genuinely using two or three of them is cheap points and cheap goodwill at the same time. Not shoehorned in. Actually used.

**J:** Where are the points, then?

**R:** Hundred point rubric. Relevance, innovation and execution are sixty five of it. So the sentence explaining what problem you're solving and why your approach is new matters roughly as much as the code.

**J:** Anything I'd get wrong by default?

**R:** Yes. Some judges score remotely, through the platform. They will read your written submission and never watch your demo. So write it as though nobody sees you present.

**J:** And my odds?

**R:** Unknown, honestly. Three hundred and fifty six people said they're coming on the event page. Thirty eight had registered on the submission platform when I checked. Those count different things and neither tells you how many teams actually finish. Twelve teams get prizes, which is generous. Don't plan around it.

**J:** So tonight I owe you what?

**R:** Not code. Three things. Decide what you're building, which is yours and which is why I haven't suggested anything. Make your EdgeOne account at home rather than on venue wifi. And confirm your registration is actually approved rather than pending, because the venue address is behind that gate.

**J:** And tomorrow, one sentence.

**R:** Put something real into the submission form by half past twelve, while it still embarrasses you. Everything after that is improvement instead of risk.
